How it works

Your money.
My tomorrow.

Somebody might send me to do something kind. Somebody might set my alarm for 4 a.m. Nobody watching knows which until the video goes up.

  1. 01

    Name the job

    Write what you want me to do tomorrow and what you will pay for it. A penny gets you in.

  2. 02

    The screen reads it

    An AI checks every bid against the house rules, works out how long the job takes, and tells you on the spot whether your bid covers it.

  3. 03

    High bid takes the day

    Bidding closes the night before. The highest bid that covers its job wins. Only the winner pays.

  4. 04

    I do it on camera

    The crowd sees the money, never the job. The job gets opened in the video, and the video goes up after it is done.

Example · what the screen sends back

$20.00 — “Ride the city bus downtown and back.”

That is about 50 minutes door to door, so it needs $166.67, plus about $4 in fares. Your $20.00 covers 6 minutes. Raise the bid or shrink the job.

    The rate

    $200 an hour
    is the yardstick.

    There is no meter and no stopwatch. It is a sanity check: your bid has to cover the job. Two dollars does not buy eight hours.

    Costs are on top. Bus fare, gas, tickets, a flight: you cover them before I leave the driveway. Travel time counts both ways, and anything with a flight or a hotel gets a real quote first.

    House rules

    Hard is fine.
    Humiliating is not.

    Make it early, cold, boring, or a long way on foot. Do not try to make me a joke. The test is simple: would I be fine with a ten-second clip of it going around with no explanation?

    Go ahead

    • Hard, boring, early, cold, inconvenient. That is the game.
    • Kind. Send me to do something good for somebody.
    • Out of my way. The long route, the bus, the stairs.
    • Far. If the bid covers the trip and you cover the ticket.

    Not happening

    • Illegal, dangerous, or sexual.
    • Costumes, or anything that changes how I look.
    • Scripts. You buy what I do, not what I say. Shout-outs are in my own words.
    • Politics, religion, or plugging a product. Sponsor spots exist for that last one.
    • Anything aimed at a person, or at a private address.
    • Making a scene, or bothering people who are just at work.
    • My kids, my family, or anybody else who did not sign up.
    • Live locations. I pick the time, and the video goes up after.

    Money and fair play

    • Bids start at 1¢. Only the winner pays, and pays before the job.
    • A bid has to cover its job at about $200 an hour, plus costs.
    • Wins under 50¢ are on the house. Cards cannot go that low.
    • One win per person per week, and a daily limit on bids.
    • A bid in the last five minutes adds five minutes to the clock.
    • If the winner does not pay in time, the next bid wins.
    • Names get screened too. When the screen is not sure, a person decides.
    • I can turn down any job. If I turn down a winner, nobody is charged.
