How it works

  1. 1

    Place a bid

    Say what you want me to do and what you will pay for it. Bids start at one cent.

  2. 2

    The highest bid wins

    Bidding closes each evening. The highest bid that covers its job wins, and only the winner pays.

  3. 3

    Watch me do it

    I do the job the next day, on camera. Nobody else sees what the job is until the video goes up.

You get an answer right away

Every bid is checked against the rules and sized up for time and cost. If your bid is too low for the job, you see what it would take.

Example

$20.00 — Ride the city bus downtown and back.

That is about 50 minutes door to door, so it needs $166.67, plus about $4 in fares. Your $20.00 covers about 6 minutes. Raise the bid or pick a smaller job.

What a bid covers

I price my time at about $200 an hour, and your bid has to be in line with the job you ask for. It is a sense check, not a timer: two dollars does not buy eight hours of work.

  • $118 seconds
  • $103 minutes
  • $5015 minutes
  • $2001 hour
  • $4002 hours

Costs are extra

Fares, fuel, tickets and flights are added on top and paid before the job. Anything with a flight or a hotel gets a real quote first.

Travel counts

The time it takes me to get there and back is part of the job, so it is part of what the bid has to cover.

The rules

A job can be hard, boring or inconvenient. It cannot be humiliating or harmful. If I would not want a ten-second clip of it shared without context, I will not do it.

Fine to ask for

  • Jobs that are hard, boring, early, cold or inconvenient
  • Kind jobs that help someone
  • Jobs that take me out of my way
  • Travel, if the bid covers the time and you cover the fare

Not allowed

  • Anything illegal, dangerous or sexual
  • Costumes, or anything that changes how I look
  • Scripts. You choose what I do, not what I say
  • Politics, religion or promoting a product
  • Anything aimed at a specific person or a private address
  • Causing a scene, or bothering people who are at work
  • Anything involving my family, or people who have not agreed to it
  • Live locations. I choose the time and post afterwards

Payment and fairness

  • Bids start at 1¢. Only the winner pays, and pays before the job.
  • A bid must cover its job at about $200 an hour, plus costs.
  • A winning bid under 50¢ is free, because cards cannot charge less.
  • One win per person per week, with a daily limit on bids.
  • A bid in the last five minutes extends bidding by five minutes.
  • If the winner does not pay in time, the next highest bid wins.
  • Names are screened too. If the screen is not sure, a person decides.
  • I can decline any job. If I decline the winning bid, nobody is charged.
