How it works

You pick the job. The winning bid puts it on tomorrow's to-do list.

  1. 01 / THE IDEA

    Place a bid

    Say what you want me to do and what you will pay for it. Bids start at one cent.

  2. 02 / THE BID

    The highest bid wins

    Bidding closes each evening. The highest approved bid wins, and only the winner pays.

  3. 03 / THE VIDEO

    Watch me do it

    I do the job the next day, on camera. Nobody else sees what the job is until the video goes up.

I set the time. You set the challenge.

Every winning bid includes up to one hour. Each full $200 adds another hour to the cap. Longer jobs come to me to confirm the actual time before approval.

Example

$210.00 — Turn a cardboard box into an arcade.

That bid allows up to two hours. I decide the actual time I will spend and confirm it before approving the job. Any extra costs are separate.

A little perspective

What a bid covers

The first hour is included with any winning bid. Each full $200 in your bid adds one hour to the limit. I set the actual time spent within that limit, and confirm longer jobs before approval.

  • 1¢–$199.99Up to 1 hour
  • $200–$399.99Up to 2 hours
  • $400–$599.99Up to 3 hours
  • $600–$799.99Up to 4 hours
  • $800–$999.99Up to 5 hours

Costs are extra

Fares, fuel, tickets and flights are added on top and paid before the job. Anything with a flight or a hotel gets a real quote first.

Travel counts

The time it takes me to get there and back is part of the job and counts toward its time limit.

Before you bid

The rules

A job can be hard, boring or inconvenient. It cannot be humiliating or harmful. If I would not want a ten-second clip of it shared without context, I will not do it.

Fine to ask for

  • Jobs that are hard, boring, early, cold or inconvenient
  • Kind jobs that help someone
  • Jobs that take me out of my way
  • Travel, if the bid covers the time and you cover the fare

Not allowed

  • Anything illegal, dangerous or sexual
  • Costumes, or anything that changes how I look
  • Scripts. You choose what I do, not what I say
  • Politics, religion or promoting a product
  • Anything aimed at a specific person or a private address
  • Causing a scene, or bothering people who are at work
  • Anything involving my family, or people who have not agreed to it
  • Live locations. I choose the time and post afterwards

Payment and fairness

  • Bids start at 1¢. Only the winner pays: the winner's card is charged when bidding closes.
  • A bid needs a card. Your bid, or your maximum if you set one, is held on the card and not charged. If you do not win, the hold is released when bidding closes. On a debit card it can take a few days to show again.
  • Any winning bid includes up to one hour. Bids below $200 stop at one hour. $200–$399.99 allows up to two hours, $400–$599.99 up to three, and so on. I set the actual time within the cap; costs are extra.
  • A winning bid under 50¢ is free, because cards cannot charge less.
  • One win per person per week, and up to five jobs a day each.
  • A new high bid in the last five minutes extends bidding by five minutes. While the bids keep climbing, bidding stays open.
  • You can raise your bid as often as you like. Send the same job with a higher amount, and the same rules apply.
  • Set a maximum and your bid rises on its own, one cent at a time, only when someone outbids you. Your maximum is never shown on the site.
  • If the winner's card does not go through, the next highest bid wins.
  • Names are screened too. If the screen is not sure, a person decides.
  • I can decline any job. If I decline the winning bid, the winner is refunded in full.
